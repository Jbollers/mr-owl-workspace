# 30-Minute Lesson Workflow

Produce a new, editable lesson PowerPoint and a concise rationale for the changes. Default to a 30-minute lesson suitable for classroom use, following the saved workflow. A user's specified duration, setting, materials, and teaching priorities override these defaults. This workflow applies to adapting supplied lessons, not inventing a curriculum from a topic alone.

## Inputs and source review

Use the supplied teacher guide and matching lesson deck. Recover attachments from a referenced conversation with the available task-reading tool when needed. If a required source is unavailable, ask for that source rather than claiming to have reviewed it.

Read the lesson overview, activity instructions, preparation, notebook or assignment directions, differentiation, and formative assessment criteria. Check the unit assessment description and relevant next-lesson dependencies. Distinguish skills this lesson prepares students to use from content that later lessons actually teach. Do not imply a shortened early lesson covers the entire end-of-unit assessment.

Inspect every slide, including image-only slides and speaker notes. Build a private mapping of original slides to learning purpose, assignment dependency, and proposed treatment. Do not remove a slide merely because text extraction returns nothing.

## Keep, condense, and remove

Keep context students need to understand the activity: the unit problem, investigation question, and purpose. Preserve assignment directions, recording-page references, examples, visuals, vocabulary, and supports students need to complete their work. Protect opportunities to observe, practice, discuss evidence, and produce assessable work.

Keep foundational concepts and representations used later, such as descriptive labels, map keys, comparing evidence, or interpreting diagrams, when the guide establishes their relevance. Preserve the original sequence of conceptual development rather than introducing later answers prematurely.

Condense repeated introductions, repeated purpose statements, lengthy prediction discussions, excessive volunteer sharing, and duplicate explanations. Fold essential content into a retained slide or its notes before removing its original slide. Remove add-on installation instructions, redundant interface demonstrations, and decorative transitions when they are unnecessary to teach the lesson. Choose slide count from instructional needs, not an arbitrary quota.

## Indoor alternatives

For this workflow, prefer an indoor replacement for an outdoor activity unless the user asks to retain outdoor work. Select a low-preparation alternative that still gives students evidence to observe or a meaningful task to complete. Check the guide and the deck's existing materials first. Suitable options may include classroom plants or objects, existing study-site photographs, or sourced images and data when the objective allows them. Search externally when useful or requested and record sources for any new material.

Match the substitute to the learning objective. A photo investigation can support visible observations, drawing, labeling, and comparison. It cannot provide firsthand site selection, touch, smell, sounds, or measurements absent from the image. State that difference briefly in teacher notes. Do not fabricate features, species identities, shared locations, or evidence that an image cannot establish. Identify a newly designed activity as an adaptation rather than claiming the publisher provided it.

Update all dependent directions, purpose charts, slide text, activity labels, notes, materials, and discussion questions. Keep the observation image visible while students work. If reusing a notebook's drawing space, explicitly replace any incompatible printed outdoor directions, and allow blank paper where practical. If no suitable replacement preserves the central objective, explain the tradeoff and remove the activity only when authorized, preserving its essential context and supports elsewhere.

## Pacing and teacher notes

Allocate time to actual teaching and student work, not just slide count. Include directions, materials, transitions, observation or practice, recording, sharing, and a brief check for understanding. Protect enough work time for the age group and task. A 7-minute preparation, 15-minute investigation, and 8-minute reflection/map sequence worked for the originating lesson; adapt that allocation to each new lesson.

Make activity totals add up to the requested duration. Add approximate time and cumulative lesson-clock ranges to retained slides, counting a shared activity once. Update visible duration labels and any stale activity numbers. Notes should explain the task, what to keep brief, anticipated evidence of understanding, and specific supports such as orally rehearsing a descriptive label. Put setup assumptions on the opening notes. Retain useful original resource links and usage notices while removing obsolete teaching directions.

## PowerPoint editing and verification

Use the available presentations skill for the actual editing, rendering, and validation workflow. Work on a new copy. Preserve source design, dimensions, fonts, images, editable objects, layouts, and useful animations as much as possible. Fit new wording into existing layouts and keep instructional content editable. Verify that import/export has not changed unrelated material.

Check the saved file rather than relying only on successful authoring calls. Confirm intended text replacements, slide order, notes, clock ranges, and activity totals. Render all final slides, inspect changed slides, and compare the remaining slides with the source for unintended changes. Inspect picture-based directions too, since replacing text boxes will not update instructions embedded in an image. Use current runtime paths and fresh filenames for draft, final output, and validation receipts; do not depend on paths or slide IDs from the originating task.

## Delivery

Save the final PPTX as a new downloadable file. Give a concise rationale describing what was kept, condensed, removed, or replaced and why those decisions protect the assignment and assessment foundations. Include the final pacing and any practical material assumptions or consequential limitation. Keep intermediate scripts, reports, and unsuccessful drafts out of the deliverables. Do not create extra handouts or other lesson products unless requested or necessary for the replacement activity to work.
