# FinalCoat

Concept art for creating 3D models.

## Game brief

A first-person psychological horror game in which a freelance painter accepts contracts through a computer portal in their apartment. Each assignment begins with ordinary work: move furniture, prepare rooms, refill the roller and completely paint the requested walls.

Subtle disturbances escalate into supernatural events: objects move, footsteps come from empty rooms, smoke forms human silhouettes, doors appear sealed and unfinished paint reveals disturbing clues. Complete the work, survive the haunting, collect payment and return home.

Between jobs, the persistent apartment hub supports messages, equipment management, earnings and a larger mystery connecting customers and their homes.

## Core loop

1. Check the apartment computer.
2. Review and accept a painting contract.
3. Gather equipment and travel to the property.
4. Inspect and prepare the work area.
5. Paint the required walls.
6. Experience increasingly disturbing events.
7. Complete the job and escape.
8. Return home, receive payment and unlock the next contract.

## Setting and apartment

A run-down urban neighborhood during an undefined modern period. Familiar, believable locations feel slightly disconnected from reality.

The modest, worn apartment contains living/dining space, a small dated kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, computer/work office, equipment storage, a suspicious sealed interior door and the contractor computer. It feels warmer and safer than job sites but never entirely comfortable. Horror gradually enters the hub.

Customer properties include an aging family apartment, neglected townhouse, empty rental, condemned building awaiting renovation, suburban home with rooms that do not match its exterior, former medical/institutional building converted to apartments, house whose previous painter disappeared and a property where new paint returns to its original condition.

## Job-site concepts

| Location | Situation |
| --- | --- |
| The Occupied Apartment | The supposedly present customer never appears. Food stays warm, a television plays elsewhere and footsteps move behind a locked door. |
| The Empty Rental | Vacant for years, according to the manager; fresh handprints appear beneath wallpaper and someone claiming to live there sends messages. |
| The Sealed Room | A chained room is excluded from the contract. Painting elsewhere triggers sounds behind its door. |
| The Repeating Hallway | Moving furniture reveals a hidden hallway whose layout changes with each entry. |
| The Child's Bedroom | Drawings show the same smoke-shaped person behind different families. Painted-over drawings reappear elsewhere. |
| The Burned Townhouse | Soot bleeds through fresh paint despite repairs. Human silhouettes appear with the lights off. |
| The Abandoned Contractor Job | Another painter's equipment remains, and their unfinished checklist updates as the player works. |

## Identity to preserve

Satisfying, methodical painting contrasted with brief, unpredictable horror that gradually contaminates both job sites and the supposedly safe apartment. Initially, players should question whether disturbances come from shadows, fumes, exhaustion or poor lighting.
